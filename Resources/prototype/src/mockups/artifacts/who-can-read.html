Design concept · client-web #7730

Who can read what I post?

Inside a space, nothing tells people whether what they write is open to the whole internet or stays among members. This page shows every place we answer that question, in the order a member runs into them. The same sentence appears everywhere, so people learn it once.

Use the switch below to see how the wording changes for each kind of space. The hardest case is a public subspace inside a private space. It is neither public nor private, so it gets its own label: Members only.

A badge next to the space name

Shown on every tab of every space and subspace. Hovering, focusing or tapping it opens one sentence naming who can read what's posted here.

"What does this mean?" opens the About dialog at the new Who can see what section. That way the badge and About lead to each other.

One line next to the Post button

In the normal Create Post dialog, in the empty space to the left of Save draft and Post, where your eyes go just before you commit. It only informs. You can't change who reads it from here, because that is a space setting.

In a public space the line gets a light blue tint. Posting to the open internet is the one case where a surprise does real harm.

The rules behind the words

A subspace's audience depends on the space above it, not only on its own setting. That is why the badge shows who can actually read, not just the setting's name. The row you're viewing is highlighted.

Decisions

Do we mention people who can see in without being members?

Admins of the space above can open a private subspace, and so can the organisation that hosts the space. Members don't know this today.

Agreed: yes, in one small line, only in the welcome dialog and About. Leave it out of the badge and the post line. Saying "only members" when that isn't quite true would cost trust if someone finds out later. The developers need to confirm exactly who this covers.

Where does "who can see your profile" go?

Joining a space puts you on its member list. Whether non-members can see that list depends on a space setting.

Agreed: only in the welcome dialog and About. That's when it matters, because it's the moment you show up on the list. Writing a post doesn't change who can see your profile, so it stays off the post line.

Should people see this before they join?

The ticket is written for "members and prospective members", but every place it lists is for people who already joined.

Agreed: keep it out of this round. The badge is visible to anyone who can open the space, which covers most of it.

Which words do we use?

"Visibility" already means something else in Alkemio: archived, demo or inactive spaces, and draft versus published posts.

Agreed: only "Public", "Private" and "Members only", and full sentences that start with who can read. Never "visibility".

Not in this round

  • Comments. The line only appears when creating a post or adding a response.
  • Telling members when a space changes from private to public. That has its own ticket (#8716).
  • The rest of the old design: who can post, who can create subspaces, how to join, licences.
  • Whiteboards shared through a guest link, which can be reached outside these rules.
