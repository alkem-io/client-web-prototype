Concept A

Header back, labelled

The breadcrumb’s parent segment, replacing the logo below md.

For

  • No new chrome; reclaims space the breadcrumb already wastes.
  • Universal — the header is on every route.
  • Says where you land. Matters at L2.
  • Reuses useBreadcrumbs().

Against

  • Costs the logo, and one-tap Home with it.
  • Label length unpredictable; truncates on long names and other locales.
  • Squeezes the icon row at 360px.

Rules

  • Below md only. Empty trail ⇒ logo.
  • Truncate around 140px, ghost style.
  • Long-press ⇒ full ancestor list.

For

  • Smallest footprint; the icon row survives intact.
  • No truncation or i18n risk.
  • Universally understood glyph.
  • Cheapest to ship and to reverse.

Against

  • Doesn’t say where you land. At L2 every chevron looks identical.
  • Still costs the logo, with nothing gained for it.
  • Lower discoverability than a labelled control.

Rules

  • Icon-only, but aria-label names the parent.
  • 44×44 target despite the 20px glyph.
  • Long-press ⇒ ancestor list.

A floating chip over the page hero. The header is never touched.

For

  • Logo, Home and the icon row all survive. Lowest-risk change to shared chrome.
  • Sits lower on screen, so easier to reach.
  • Fits the existing overlayHeader banner treatment.

Against

  • Not universal — banner-less routes need a fallback, reintroducing B’s two-patterns problem.
  • Scrolls away unless pinned; pinning re-creates a floating element over content.
  • Contrast unpredictable over user-uploaded banners.

Rules

  • Frosted white for legibility over arbitrary imagery.
  • Banner-less routes fall back to A or G.
  • Sticks to the header on scroll.

Ellipsis tapped · full trail

For

  • Only concept that keeps one-tap Home and adds back — the logo stays as the first crumb.
  • Every ancestor is reachable, not just the parent. Escapes an L2 dead end in one tap.
  • Restores parity with desktop instead of inventing a mobile-only pattern.
  • Ships the collapse logic AppBreadcrumb already has; nothing new to invent.
  • Universal — the header is on every route.

Against

  • At two or more levels, going up one is two taps — open, then choose.
  • Crumbs are small targets; the trail competes with the icon row for width.
  • Reads as orientation, not as an action. Users hunting for “back” may not see a button.
  • An ellipsis is a weak, low-discoverability affordance.
  • Truncation is worst here — two user-generated names in one row.

Rules

  • Below md only. Empty trail ⇒ logo alone.
  • Two segments render inline; three or more collapse the middle to an ellipsis.
  • Logo crumb is always Home; current crumb is never a link.
  • Ellipsis and each crumb are 44×44 targets despite the visual size.
  • Dropdown lists the complete trail, current item marked.

For

  • Fits the real constraint — three levels in ~62px, so nothing in the icon row has to move.
  • Keeps everything I gives: one-tap Home, every ancestor reachable, one tap up from any depth.
  • Immune to long names and translation — no text to overflow.
  • Uses BreadcrumbTrailItem.avatar, which already exists in production.
  • Space avatars are already how users recognise spaces elsewhere in the product.

Against

  • Recognition depends on avatar quality. Spaces without one fall back to initials, which are weak at 16px.
  • Two visually similar banners next to each other are indistinguishable.
  • A row of small squares reads as decoration; discoverability is the lowest of any concept.
  • 16px glyphs need invisible 44px targets, so they cannot sit tightly packed.
  • Screen readers carry the whole burden — labels must be exact.

Rules

  • Avatar from the space’s card banner; initials fallback on a tinted chip.
  • Up to three glyphs inline; beyond that the middle collapses to an ellipsis.
  • Current crumb carries a ring and is not tappable.
  • Each glyph is a 44×44 target with an aria-label naming the space.
  • Tapping any crumb navigates directly; tapping the current one opens the labelled list.
